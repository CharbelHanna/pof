# Templates

The prep you would otherwise rebuild from scratch every time — a shot list and
the checklists that go with it, kept so you can drop them onto the next job.

## When to use one

You shoot newborn sessions every other week. The shot list barely changes, the
kit never changes, and you type both out again every time. That is the
situation this exists for.

The test is whether you have ever thought _I did this exact setup last month_.
If a kind of shoot comes round more than twice a year, it is worth a template.

## Making one

The useful way is to save a session that already worked. Nobody sits down and
writes a template from nothing, and you do not have to.

1. Open the shoot, and find the session in the list.
2. Under its details, press **Save as template**.
3. Give it a name — "Standard newborn", "Studio headshots" — and press
   **Save**.

It confirms what it took: _Saved as "Standard newborn", with 11 items._

### What gets saved

- **The shots** on that session, and any shots on the shoot that are not tied
  to a particular day. That second part matters, because shots are created
  without a day by default.
- **Every checklist on the shoot**, with all their items — not only the ones
  attached to that session. A gear list usually belongs to the whole job, and
  a template with a shot list and no kit list would be half a template.

What does **not** get saved: dates, times, locations, crew, references, and
whether anything was ticked or captured. A template is the structure, not the
particular day.

## Applying one

1. Open the shoot and find the session you want to fill.
2. Under its details, choose from **Apply a template…**.

It tells you what arrived: _Added 7 shots and 4 checklist items._

The shots land on that session. The checklists land on the shoot.

### Applying adds, it does not replace

Anything already on the shoot stays. New shots are added after the ones that
are already there, so you can apply a template on top of work in progress
without losing it.

You can apply more than one template to the same session — a "Standard
newborn" and a "Winter outdoor extras", say — and you get both.

**Checklists with the same name are merged, not duplicated.** If two templates
both want a Gear list called "Camera bag", you end up with one camera bag
holding everything. That is deliberate: two lists called "Camera bag" is
exactly how something gets left behind.

## Seeing what is in one

Press **Templates** in the menu, then the template's name.

You get the shots it will add and the checklists it will create, laid out as
they will appear on a shoot rather than as a flat list. Worth a look before
applying one to a real job, particularly if you made it months ago.

## Things worth knowing

### Editing a shoot does not change the template, and editing a template does not change the shoot

Applying a template **copies** it. Once the work is on your shoot it is yours
— rename the shots, delete half of them, reorder the lot. Nothing propagates
back.

The same holds in the other direction, which matters more. A shoot you ran in
January keeps exactly what it was given, for good. Your records of a finished
job will not quietly change underneath you.

### Deleting a template leaves your shoots alone

Shoots already built from it keep everything. The template simply stops being
offered.

### A template cannot be changed once saved

There is no way to edit a template's contents in this version. If the shot
list has moved on, save the newer session as a new template and delete the old
one.

Likewise, the **Add template** button on the Templates page only creates an
empty one, and there is currently no way to fill it in — it will show _Nothing
saved in it yet_ and cannot be applied. Until that changes, make templates by
saving a session.

### An empty template is never offered

If a template has nothing in it, it does not appear in the **Apply a
template…** list at all. That is on purpose: a template that applied and did
nothing would look like the app was broken.
