# Crew and gear

Who is coming, and what is going in the van.

Both sit on the shoot page.

## Crew

Add a **Crew role** and optionally a **Crew name**.

The role is required and the name is not, which matches how a job actually
comes together: you know you need a stylist three weeks before you know which
stylist. Add the role now, fill in the name when you have booked someone.

Common roles are suggested — Photographer, Assistant, Second shooter, Hair and
makeup, Stylist, Producer, Digitech, Model, Client — but you can type anything.
Every genre has its own job titles and the list is not meant to be exhaustive.

## Gear

Gear works differently from crew, because the same lens turns up on a hundred
shoots and should be the same lens every time.

### Your kit library

The first time you add a piece of equipment you give it a name and optionally
a category — Camera, Lens, Lighting, Modifier, Support, Power, Storage, Audio,
Grip or Other. It joins your kit library.

After that it is picked from **Add from your kit…** rather than typed again.
So "85mm f/1.4" means one specific lens across every shoot, rather than six
slightly different spellings.

There is no separate setup step for this. The library fills itself as you plan
— add new equipment from **Or add new kit** whenever you first need it, and it
is both on this shoot and in the library from then on.

### Packing

Each item on a shoot has a checkbox. Tick it when it is in the bag. The count
at the top reads "6 of 11 packed".

## Gear versus a gear checklist

Both exist and they are not the same thing. It is worth being clear about
which you want.

**Gear** is your actual equipment, picked from a library you build up over
time. It knows that your 85mm is one object that appears on many jobs. Use it
when you want to say _which_ equipment this shoot needs.

**A gear [checklist](checklists.md)** is a list of lines you type. It does not
know anything about your kit and does not have to — a line can say "spare
gaffer tape" or "ask Tom to bring the ladder".

In practice many people use the gear list for the expensive, countable things
and a Gear checklist for everything else. Neither is wrong, and using both is
normal.

## Things worth knowing

- Crew and gear belong to the whole shoot, not to an individual session.
- Removing a piece of equipment from a shoot does not remove it from your kit
  library — it stays available for the next job.
