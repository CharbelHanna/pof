# On-set mode

A stripped-back screen for use while you are actually shooting.

Everything else in Point of Focus is for planning: at a desk, with two hands,
with time to think. On-set mode is the one screen designed for the opposite —
outdoors, one-handed, often in the dark, with a camera in the other hand.

## When to use it

Switch to it when you stop planning and start shooting. Walking to the
location is about the right moment.

It answers one question — _what have I not got yet?_ — and deliberately
refuses to answer any others. You cannot rename a shoot or edit a location
from here. That is not an oversight; it is why the screen is usable while you
are working.

## Getting there

Open the shoot and press **On set**, the orange button beside the shoot name
at the top of the page.

To get back, press **← Back to planning**.

## What it does differently

### The screen stays on

Your phone will not lock itself while this screen is open.

This matters more than it sounds. A phone that locks every thirty seconds
means unlocking it with cold or wet hands, one-handed, every time you want to
tick something off — which is how people give up and go back to paper.

The top right says which is happening: **Screen staying on**, or **Screen will
sleep** if your phone refused. Battery saver mode will refuse, and so will
some older phones. It is stated plainly rather than hidden so you know whether
you can put the phone down and trust it.

### Finished work disappears

Once a shot is marked, it leaves the list. What is in front of you is what is
still to get, not a list you have to re-read to find your place in.

Nothing is lost. Press **Show N finished** to see what you have already dealt
with, and **Undo** on anything you marked too early.

The same applies to checklists: only unfinished lists appear, under **Still to
do**, and a list you have completely finished vanishes from this screen.

## Working through the shots

Each shot shows its title, and underneath it the framing and subjects if you
filled those in, then the description. It is all readable without tapping
anything — on set you need to know what the frame is supposed to be, and
opening a panel one-handed while holding a camera is not realistic.

Two buttons:

- **Got it** — you have the shot.
- **Skip** — you are not getting this one. The light went, the location was
  shut, the client changed their mind.

**Skip is a decision, not a deletion.** The shot stays on the record as
deliberately skipped, which is worth having afterwards when someone asks why
there are no shots of the east wing. It does not count towards your captured
total.

The counter at the top reads "2 of 7 captured", and tells you how many are
left to go.

## Working through checklists

Under **Still to do**, tap anywhere on a row to tick it off — the whole row is
the target, not just the small box. One-handed, in the dark, aiming at a tiny
square is the difference between using the list and abandoning it.

Items tick off and vanish from this screen. To untick something you will need
to go back to the planning page.

## Filtering by session

If the shoot has more than one session, a dropdown at the top lets you narrow
to one of them, or show **All shots**.

Useful on a multi-day job: on Tuesday morning you want Tuesday's shots, not
the whole week's.

## Things worth knowing

- Everything you do here is the same information as the planning page, not a
  separate copy. A shot marked **Got it** on set shows as `captured` when you
  go back, and a checklist item ticked here is ticked there.
- It works on a phone, a tablet or a laptop, but the layout and the tap
  targets are built for a phone held in one hand.
- If you have no shots on the shoot yet, there is nothing for this screen to
  show. Build the [shot list](shot-lists.md) first.
