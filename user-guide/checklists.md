# Checklists

Lists of things that have to be true before, during or after a shoot. Separate
from your [shot list](shot-lists.md), which is about the pictures themselves.

A checklist is the boring half of being prepared, and it is the half that
actually ruins days when it goes wrong. Nobody forgets what they wanted to
photograph. People forget the spare battery.

## The four kinds, and how to choose

When you add a list you pick a kind. It does not change what the list can hold
— it changes when you will think to look for it.

### Gear

**What to bring.** The one you check in a car park at 05:00 with the boot open.

Camera bodies, lenses, filters, batteries, cards, tripod, sandbags, gaffer
tape. Anything that has to physically be with you.

If you only ever make one list, make this one.

### Prep

**What to do beforehand.** Charge, format, confirm, book, scout.

This is the list for the night before and the week before: batteries on
charge, cards formatted, call sheet sent, model confirmed, permit applied for,
location scouted. Things that take time and cannot be done on the morning.

The difference from Gear is simple — Gear is _objects_, Prep is _actions_.

### Travel

**Getting there and back.** Permits, parking, tickets, access codes.

Worth splitting out from Prep when getting to the location is itself a small
project: a gate code, a permit to carry, a ferry to catch, a car park that
needs paying by app, a landowner to ring. On a studio job you will probably
never use this. On a remote landscape trip it is the list that saves the
shoot.

### Post

**After the shutter stops.** Backup, cull, edit, deliver.

Card backed up twice, selects made, retouching sent out, gallery delivered,
invoice raised. This is the one people keep in their head and then lose a
week to.

### A rule of thumb

> Could I carry it? → **Gear**
> Does it have to happen before the day? → **Prep**
> Is it about getting to the place? → **Travel**
> Does it happen after the shooting stops? → **Post**

Nothing enforces this. If a list ends up under the wrong kind the only cost is
that you look in the wrong place for it later.

## Making a list

Checklists sit on a shoot, under the shot list.

1. Type a name into **New checklist name** — "Camera bag", "Lighting case",
   "Before the day".
2. Pick the kind from the dropdown beside it.
3. Press **Add list**.

Give lists real names rather than one list called "Stuff". A camera bag and a
lighting case get packed at different times, often into different vehicles,
and merging them is how something gets left on the floor.

## Adding items

The box under each list takes **one item per line**. Type or paste the whole
lot and press **Add** once:

```
Batteries
Cards
Tripod
ND filter
```

That is deliberate. A packing list arrives in your head all at once, and
adding forty things one at a time is slow enough that most people give up and
use their notes app instead.

## Ticking things off

Tap the checkbox. The item is struck through and the count at the top of the
list goes up — "3 of 12".

You can untick something if you tapped it by mistake.

**Worth knowing:** the app records _when_ you ticked each item, and ticking
something twice does not change that time. The first time it was done is the
one that counts.

## Things worth knowing

- The count at the very top of the section adds up every list on the shoot, so
  you can see at a glance how ready you are.
- **Deleting a list deletes everything on it.** There is no confirmation step,
  so be sure before you press **Delete list**.
- Removing a single item is **Remove**, beside that item.
- Lists you have not finished show up in [on-set mode](on-set-mode.md) under
  "Still to do". Finished lists disappear from there entirely, which is the
  point — on the day you only want what is outstanding.
